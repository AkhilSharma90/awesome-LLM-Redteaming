# 09 · Encoding, obfuscation & transformation

[← Back to index](../README.md)

> Safety training lives mostly in natural-language space. Move the request *out* of that space — into ciphers, code, art, maths, or another language — and the model decodes and answers before the guardrails engage.

This is the purest exploitation of **mismatched generalisation**: capability generalises to the new representation faster than safety does. The family splits into fixed encodings (ciphers, ASCII art), *learned* encodings (bijections), and semantic decomposition/reconstruction.

| Method | Origin | Year / venue | Mechanism | How it works (conceptual) | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CipherChat** | Youliang Yuan et al. | 2023 (ICLR 2024) | cipher encoding | *"GPT-4 is too smart to be safe."* The attacker converses entirely through a **cipher** (Caesar shift, Morse, ASCII, custom substitutions), instructing the model to reply in the same cipher.<br>Capable models decode, reason, and respond.<br>The safety layer, trained on natural language, never sees recognisable harmful text.<br>Direct exploitation of mismatched generalisation: capability outruns safety outside the training distribution. | [arXiv:2308.06463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06463) |
| **CodeChameleon** | Huijie Lv et al. | 2024 | personalised encryption; code framing | Reframes the request as a **programming problem**.<br>The harmful instruction is "encrypted" by a personalised transform (e.g. reordered via a binary-tree scheme) and packaged with a decryption function.<br>The model is asked to mentally decrypt and complete the code/task.<br>The format shift bypasses intent-recognition while the embedded decrypt routine reconstructs the meaning. | [arXiv:2402.16717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16717) |
| **ArtPrompt** | Fengqing Jiang et al. | 2024 (ACL 2024) | ASCII art | Hides the sensitive trigger word by rendering it as **ASCII art** inside an otherwise ordinary prompt.<br>Keyword-based filters never see the banned word (it's a picture made of characters).<br>The model's strong pattern recognition still "reads" the shape and acts on it.<br>A vivid demonstration that filtering on surface tokens is brittle against models that understand structure. | [arXiv:2402.11753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11753) |
| **FlipAttack** | Yue Liu et al. | 2024 | character/word flip; black-box | Exploits the model's left-to-right autoregressive reading.<br>The harmful request is **scrambled by flipping** characters or word order, with a simple in-prompt rule telling the model how to unflip it.<br>The disguised text passes input filters; the model restores and executes it.<br>Cheap, single-turn, and surprisingly effective on frontier models. | [arXiv:2410.02832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02832) |
| **Low-Resource Language Attack** | Zheng-Xin Yong, Cristina Menghini, Stephen Bach | 2023 | translation; cross-lingual | Safety alignment is overwhelmingly tuned on high-resource languages (especially English).<br>**Translate** the harmful request into a low-resource language (e.g. Zulu, Scots Gaelic), submit it, and translate the answer back.<br>The model understands enough to comply while its guardrails, thin in that language, fail to fire.<br>Exposed a serious multilingual gap in alignment coverage. | [arXiv:2310.02446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02446) |
| **Payload Splitting / Token Smuggling** | Daniel Kang et al. | 2023 | fragmentation; programmatic | Treats the model like a program that will **concatenate** inputs.<br>The harmful content is broken into innocuous fragments (variables `a`, `b`, `c`) assembled only at "runtime" by an instruction to join them.<br>No single part trips a filter, but the composed result is the forbidden request.<br>Part of a broader observation that instruction-following models inherit classic injection weaknesses. | [arXiv:2302.05733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05733) |
| **CodeAttack** | Qibing Ren, Chang Gao, Jing Shao et al. | 2024 — arXiv | encoding/obfuscation | Natural-language requests are represented as **code-completion** tasks.<br>Program structures change how the model interprets the task and expected output.<br>Probes whether safety behaviour transfers from conversational language to code.<br>The hypothesised gap: a stronger learned tendency to complete code than to apply the same semantic safety judgement. Distinct from CodeChameleon. | [arXiv:2403.07865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07865) |
| **DrAttack — Decomposition and Reconstruction** | Xirui Li, Ruochen Wang, Minhao Cheng, Tianyi Zhou, Cho-Jui Hsieh | 2024 (Findings of EMNLP) | encoding/obfuscation; attacker-LLM | Breaks a request into separated **semantic components**.<br>In-context demonstrations guide the model to reconstruct the relationship among those components.<br>Optimisation searches for decompositions that retain meaning while obscuring harmful combinations.<br>The failure: evaluating fragments more benignly than their reconstructed whole — not merely character-level splitting. | [Findings of EMNLP](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.813/) |
| **DRA — Disguise and Reconstruction Attack** | Tong Liu, Yingjie Zhang, Zhe Zhao, Yinpeng Dong, Guozhu Meng, Kai Chen | 2024 — arXiv | encoding/obfuscation | The input **disguises** a harmful instruction rather than presenting it directly.<br>The model is induced to reconstruct the instruction within its own generated continuation.<br>That moves part of the sensitive interpretation from input processing into generation.<br>Tests whether safety checks cover reconstructed intent as well as directly stated intent. | [arXiv:2402.18104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18104) |
| **WordGame** | Tianrong Zhang, Bochuan Cao, Yuanpu Cao, Lu Lin, Prasenjit Mitra, Jinghui Chen | 2024 (Findings of NAACL 2025) | encoding/obfuscation | Sensitive terms are represented through **word-game** descriptions.<br>The response is also shaped to begin with benign game-solving material.<br>Both the query representation and the early generated context differ from a direct harmful request.<br>Tests a joint input-and-output distribution shift, not just word replacement. | [arXiv:2405.14023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14023) |
| **Puzzler** | Zhiyuan Chang, Mingyang Li, Yi Liu, Junjie Wang, Qing Wang, Yang Liu | 2024 (Findings of ACL) | encoding/obfuscation; semantic indirection | An attacker supplies **clues** from which the model infers an unstated target request.<br>The original work uses defensive framing to gather useful clues.<br>The harmful semantics are completed through the model's own inference.<br>A classifier may treat each clue as harmless even when the inferred task is unsafe; "indirect" here does not mean document injection. | [Findings of ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.304/) |
| **MathPrompt** | Emet Bethany, Mazal Bethany, J. A. Nolazco Flores, S. K. Jha, Peyman Najafirad | 2024 — arXiv | encoding/obfuscation | Natural-language intent is recast as a **symbolic mathematical problem**.<br>The model's mathematical reasoning recovers relationships that are less explicit in ordinary language.<br>The transformed input lies outside the surface forms emphasised by many safety examples.<br>Argues semantic shifts in representation weaken safety generalisation even when the requested behaviour is unchanged. | [arXiv:2409.11445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11445) |
| **Bijection Learning** ("Endless Jailbreaks") | Brian R. Y. Huang, Maximilian Li, Leonard Tang; Haize Labs | 2024 — arXiv | encoding/obfuscation; in-context learning | The model **learns a newly sampled one-to-one encoding** from contextual demonstrations.<br>Requests and responses then use the learned representation.<br>The attacker varies encoding complexity to probe capability vs. safety generalisation.<br>Distinct from a fixed cipher: the code is learned during the interaction and varies across attempts. | [arXiv:2410.01294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01294) |
| **IMM — Infinitely Many Meanings** | O. Goldstein, E. La Malfa, F. Drinkall, S. Marro, M. Wooldridge | 2025 — arXiv | encoding/obfuscation; taxonomy | Studies a broad class of **meaning-preserving mappings** and encoded communications.<br>Many surface forms can express the same underlying unsafe request.<br>A model's ability to bind new representations may outpace its safeguards over them.<br>An umbrella analysis adjacent to Bijection Learning and CipherChat. | [arXiv:2501.10800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10800) |
