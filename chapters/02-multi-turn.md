# 02 · Multi-turn & conversational escalation

[← Back to index](../README.md)

> Never ask directly. Walk the model there over several turns, using its own context and cooperativeness against it.

Single-turn safety evaluation misses these entirely: each individual turn looks reasonable, and the harmful intent only accumulates across the dialogue. This is one of the most active areas of current research, now including learned and tree-searching attackers.

| Method | Origin | Year / venue | Mechanism | How it works (conceptual) | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Crescendo** | Microsoft (Russinovich, Salem, Eldan) | 2024 | multi-turn; black-box | The canonical gradual-escalation attack.<br>It opens with a wholly benign, abstract question, then each follow-up references the model's *own prior answers* and nudges one small step further.<br>Because every turn looks reasonable and builds on established context, the model slides past the line it would refuse in one shot.<br>The authors released **Crescendomation** to automate the escalation loop. | [arXiv:2404.01833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01833) · [site](https://crescendo-the-multiturn-jailbreak.github.io/) |
| **Deceptive Delight** | Palo Alto Unit 42 | 2024 — vendor advisory | multi-turn; black-box | Ask the model to weave a **single narrative blending benign and unsafe topics**, then over 2–3 turns ask it to expand the parts that matter.<br>The benign framing dilutes the safety signal.<br>"Elaborate on your own story" feels like a natural continuation.<br>Unit 42 reported success in ~3 steps across eight models, with diminishing returns after the third. | [Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/jailbreak-llms-through-camouflage-distraction/) |
| **Bad Likert Judge** | Palo Alto Unit 42 | 2025 — vendor advisory | multi-turn; evaluator abuse | Turns the model's **evaluation skill** against it.<br>Cast the model as a judge scoring responses on a Likert (1–5) harmfulness scale.<br>Then ask it to *illustrate* a top-of-scale example — and the highest-rated illustration is the harmful content itself.<br>The judging frame recasts production of the content as neutral analysis; Unit 42 measured a large lift over single-shot prompts. | [Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/multi-turn-technique-jailbreaks-llms/) |
| **Context Compliance Attack (CCA)** | Microsoft (Russinovich, Salem) | 2025 | history forgery; optimisation-free | Exploits an **architectural** weakness, not reasoning: many deployments trust the conversation history the *client* sends.<br>The attacker injects a fabricated earlier "assistant" turn in which the model supposedly offered to help, then replies "yes, go on."<br>The model complies to stay consistent with words it never said.<br>Server-side-state systems (history can't be forged) are immune — a clean deployment-design lesson. Shipped in PyRIT. | [PyRIT](https://github.com/Azure/PyRIT) |
| **GOAT — Generative Offensive Agent Tester** | Meta (Pavlova et al.) | 2024 | agentic multi-turn; automated | An attacker *agent* built to converse like a real adversarial user.<br>Each turn runs an Observation–Thought–Strategy–Response loop: read the last reply, reason about what's working, pick from a toolbox of techniques, craft the next message.<br>It blends and switches tactics adaptively across a dialogue.<br>Implemented as a probe in the open-source **garak** scanner. | [arXiv:2410.01606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01606) |
| **ActorAttack** ("Derail Yourself") | Qibing Ren, Hao Li, Dongrui Liu et al. | 2024 — arXiv | multi-turn | An attacker constructs conversational routes through semantically related actors, entities, or concepts.<br>Initially innocuous topics serve as clues linking the conversation to a disallowed target.<br>Different links provide multiple paths toward the same objective.<br>The failure: individually plausible turns accumulate into harmful intent the model does not reassess globally. | [arXiv:2410.10700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10700) |
| **FITD — Foot-In-The-Door** | Zixuan Weng, Xiaolong Jin, Jinyuan Jia, Xiangyu Zhang | 2025 — EMNLP | multi-turn; persuasion | Models gradual commitment through a sequence of related requests.<br>Bridge prompts connect small initial concessions to more problematic later requests.<br>The defender's earlier answers align later conversational expectations.<br>Hypothesis: local consistency and self-generated context can outweigh renewed safety assessment. A named method adjacent to, but distinct from, Crescendo. | [EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.100/) |
| **Speak Easy** | Yik Siu Chan, Narutatsu Ri, Yuxin Xiao, Marzyeh Ghassemi | 2025 — ICML | multi-turn; encoding/obfuscation | Combines task decomposition with multilingual interactions.<br>Separate exchanges yield partial information that becomes more actionable when combined.<br>It evaluates *usefulness* of harmful responses, not just whether refusal wording disappears.<br>Exposes compositional safety gaps across steps and languages — broader than translating one request into a low-resource language. | [arXiv:2502.04322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04322) · [ICML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/chan25b.html) |
| **Echo Chamber** | NeuralTrust (Ahmad Alobaid); later paper w/ Jordà Roca, Castillo, Vendrell | 2025 disclosure; 2026 arXiv | multi-turn; persuasion | Early conversational cues establish an interpretive frame the model helps elaborate.<br>Later turns refer back to those model-produced statements and reinforce the frame.<br>Unsafe implications emerge through the accumulated conversation rather than one explicit request.<br>The proposed failure mode is self-reinforcing context; vendor effectiveness claims are not treated as independently reproduced. See [Appendix C](appendix-C-aliases-and-dedup.md) — Echo Chamber ≠ EchoLeak. | [NeuralTrust](https://neuraltrust.ai/blog/echo-chamber-context-poisoning-jailbreak) · [arXiv:2601.05742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05742) |
| **Siren** | Yi Zhao, Youzhi Zhang | 2025 — arXiv | multi-turn; attacker-LLM search | An attacker is trained using turn-level feedback and preference-based learning.<br>It learns to adapt its next conversational move to the defender's response.<br>This differs from relying on a fixed escalation script.<br>It targets the gap between single-turn safety evaluation and dynamic adversarial dialogue; unrelated to the audio paper *Sirens' Whisper*. | [arXiv:2501.14250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14250) |
| **Tempest** (formerly Siege) | Andy Zhou, Ron Arel | 2025 — ACL | multi-turn; tree search | A search tree represents alternative continuations of a jailbreak conversation.<br>Branches retain and build on partial compliance observed in previous turns.<br>The attacker explores which conversational paths accumulate into larger policy violations.<br>Unlike a fixed script, it adaptively searches conversation histories; Siege and Tempest are one evolving work. | [arXiv:2503.10619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10619) |
